## The net-cash judgment (load-bearing)

Naive net cash = $1,384.5M − $6.4M = **+$1,378M**. **That is the wrong input for a PT model**:
$1,043.7M of warrant liabilities settle in **shares**, so crediting the full cash pile to equity
while using the current share count double-counts them — the NBIS precedent ("converts + prefunded
warrants are why `share_count_M` ramps").

Model uses **warrant-adjusted net cash = $1,384.5 − $6.4 − $1,043.7 = +$334M**, with the share
count ramped 600M → 650M → 700M.

**Corroboration:** TipRanks publishes EV **$3.46B**. Naive net cash implies EV $2.85B; the
warrant-adjusted figure implies **$3.90B**. TipRanks sits *between* them — i.e. they deduct part
of the derivative liability. The adjusted treatment brackets the third-party number far better
than the naive one, which is why it is the base case and the naive case is carried as sensitivity.
